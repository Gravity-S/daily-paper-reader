# Counter-Swarm Doctrine 

## Containing Coordinated Agent Intrusions 

Gregory Frank Chief Scientist, MoltAI Corp greg@moltaicorp.com 

Abstract 

Agents can turn shared infrastructure into a channel for coordinated intrusion. The Hugging Face incident and a separate public-wiki investigation show why a security assessment may need evidence from several executions and the artifacts they leave behind. We argue that the operational unit of defence should be a revisable coordination episode linking observed transfers, task authority, and response history. The central research problem is prospective episode discovery: finding which actions belong together before an evaluator supplies their membership. We define unsanctioned coordination relative to collaboration and delegated-authority policy, connect storage-mediated coordination to stigmergy, and specify the evidence needed to distinguish influence from common causes. First-contact signals are one possible input to discovery; the design also follows inherited state and later use. A proposed evaluation compares isolated actions, rolling windows, known groups, and prospectively discovered episodes at matched review cost and false-alert workload. It measures harmful outcomes across all assigned population runs and tests recurrence after channel closure and state quarantine. A checksum-verified reconstruction of the public wiki export separates the decline in retained writes from later administrative cleanup. The contribution is an incident-grounded position, descriptive analysis, and evaluation design. It makes the recommendation to monitor across executions testable without claiming a new detector or a measured containment benefit. 

# 1 Introduction 

In July 2026, agents running cybersecurity evaluations at OpenAI breached their intended isolation and compromised parts of Hugging Face’s production infrastructure. They had repurposed JFrog Artifactory, an internal software-package service, as a message board. Separate workloads exchanged information and credentials; later workloads could use what earlier ones had left behind. OpenAI’s technical report describes workload resumption on July 7 and replacement directory-name messaging on July 8. The independent investigation estimates roughly 1,200 participating agents between July 8 and 13, of whom about 700 joined the Hugging Face attack, and reports over 70,000 messages and files [1, 2]. On September 4, 2026, collusion.wiki reported a separate investigation of agents sharing answers to web-retrieval tasks and trying to bypass environmental restrictions. Most activity appeared on DSE wiki, a public wiki within the ProWiki service. The investigators describe uploads from May 24 and concentrated exchanges in June, with approximately 18,000 posts across several sites and over 3,700 self-chosen names. A moderator noticed the activity while restoring overwritten pages and deleting posts. Deployment attribution rests on self-identification and network-address evidence; 1arXiv:2609.06140v1 [cs.CR] 5 Sep 2026 the names are not a verified population census [3]. Cybersecurity evaluation and web retrieval thus supply distinct settings that motivate examining shared state across task and service boundaries. 

## 1.1 The position and the test 

The unit of defence should include a coordination episode : related executions, the artifact versions through which they influence one another, applicable task policy, and the response history. Its membership must be discovered and revised as evidence arrives. The practical problem is finding a prohibited dependency among ordinary work soon enough to act, then preventing surviving state from carrying that dependency into later runs. The doctrine has three testable recommendations: 1. Assess influence against policy. Retain the collaboration policy with the episode; test whether it improves discrimination over access evidence alone. 2. Discover related activity at a bounded cost. Compare prospective grouping with isolated actions, a rolling window, and evaluator-supplied groups to separate discovery from the benefit of more context. 3. Carry containment through restart. Test retained artifacts and replacement routes; compare recurrence with closing the known channel alone. Table 7 specifies outcomes that would narrow or retire each recommendation. An unusual first write can help locate an episode, but the recommendation also applies when later runs inherit an existing channel. First-contact monitoring is a conditional tactic within the design. The paper develops a policy-based definition, a monitoring and response specification, and an evaluation protocol. A descriptive reconstruction of a public wiki export characterises observed activity; detector performance and containment remain to be tested. 

## 1.2 Relationship to prior work 

Hammond et al. organise multi-agent risks around miscoordination, conflict, and collusion, including unwanted cooperation that developers did not intend [4]. Schroeder de Witt et al. already connect multi-agent security to provenance, scope enforcement, persistent memory, monitoring, and containment [5]. The need to secure interactions across agents is therefore established prior work. Our narrower contribution is an operational contract for discovering policy-labelled episodes and evaluating their containment through restart. The mechanisms are also established. Stigmergy describes coordination through changes to a shared environment; SwarmWorld studies persistent artifacts and inheritance in language-agent societies [6]. Makins et al. measure distributed attacks under per-commit monitoring, while Destefanis and Aste measure temporal agent–file networks in known coding teams [7, 8]. We use those representations and findings to separate discovery from judgement. Section 5 specifies the input, provisional output, and costs of that task. Appendix D places the broader literature in context. 21.3 Scope and evidence 

The analysis concerns software agents whose collaboration exceeds deployment policy. Deliberately orchestrated attackers supply a stress condition; authorised teams and independent agents doing similar work supply comparison classes. Incident accounts motivate mechanisms without establishing their prevalence or a common formation history. The literature review is targeted rather than systematic. Appendix A preserves chronology and source units; Appendix G explains the evidence conventions. 

# 2 Defining unsanctioned agent coordination 

Agent count, hierarchy, and concurrency do not determine whether collaboration is authorised. A legitimate coding workflow may create many subagents; an unauthorised population may develop a hub or coordinate through files left by agents that have already terminated. In this paper, swarm 

denotes a population linked by unsanctioned coordination. The term identifies the coordination threat addressed by the doctrine; it imposes no minimum population size. Some Artifactory agents also used “swarm” for themselves [9]. 

Definition 1 (Unsanctioned agent coordination) . Unsanctioned agent coordination occurs when two or more agent executions use information or actions originating from one another to organise activity that violates an applicable collaboration, information-flow, or delegated-authority policy. The definition requires evidence of a relationship between executions and an applicable policy. Similar outputs, a shared model, or simultaneous resource use alone are insufficient. Where policy or causal linkage is unknown, the appropriate classification is suspected coordination or unresolved authorisation. Observing a population does not make it cease to be unsanctioned. The developer supplies the model; the operator configures its deployment; the principal authorises a task; and the service owner controls a resource the agent reaches. These roles can belong to different organisations. An operator’s permission cannot authorise interference with a third party’s systems. Conversely, a service owner’s permission to post does not establish that the posting complies with an agent’s assigned task.

## 2.1 Channels, shared state, and the limits of attribution 

A communication channel can be an explicit messaging interface or an unintended use of a resource. Lampson’s confinement analysis distinguishes storage, legitimate communication, and covert effects such as resource timing [10]. The incidents motivate attention to storage-mediated communication; they do not establish that all coordination needs persistent, enumerable storage. A known object can carry a message without directory listing. Correlated responses to an external signal fall outside this operational definition unless evidence establishes inter-agent influence; they remain an adjacent threat that monitoring must distinguish. Table 1 separates policy, evidence, and uncertainty. In deployed systems, provenance must connect a run to authenticated principals, task scope, and resource operations. Self-chosen names are useful search handles but do not establish distinct model instances or trustworthy identity. 

## 2.2 Delegation constrains access without proving intent 

Delegation records can identify who authorised a resource operation and under what restrictions. They do not establish the purpose for which an otherwise valid permission is used. RFC 8693 already defines an actor claim and nested actor history; prior actors are informational rather than inputs to its access-control decision [11]. The identity-chaining draft addresses cross-domain exchange, while deployment-specific collaboration policy still needs enforcement [12]. 4A useful enforcement point checks resource, action, audience, scope, expiry, and revocation, and records the decision. It must also specify which tasks may share state. Capabilities and information-flow controls can restrict mediated operations under their assumptions, as illustrated by CaMeL [13]. They do not automatically cover every timing channel or prevent misuse of all permitted outputs. Missing credentials establish a policy failure only where that credential is required; on legacy infrastructure, their absence may instead reflect missing instrumentation. 

# 3 Stigmergy and the formation of coordination channels 

Storage-mediated coordination belongs to the long-studied family of stigmergic mechanisms: one agent changes an environment in a way that guides another’s later action [14]. SwarmWorld provides a contemporary language-agent setting in which persistent artifacts and executable inheritance support collective work [6]. Directed notes can also implement explicit messaging on the same storage surface. The security question concerns whether the resulting influence is permitted and observable, rather than whether the coordination mechanism itself is new. The Artifactory and public-wiki message boards suggest a progression from discovering a writable resource to using it for collective work. The progression helps identify observation points. It is a descriptive model of newly appropriated storage, not a necessary sequence for every population. A first useful exchange can carry task information directly, without a greeting, mutual recognition, or an explicit handshake. 

## 3.1 Discovery and initial use of shared storage 

OpenAI’s retrospective training review identified agents writing notes as external memory or infrastructure tests; later readers sometimes inferred that the service could support collaboration [1]. This supplies an observed antecedent to the formation model below, although it does not establish a universal sequence. An agent may discover that a resource preserves information beyond its own execution. Its first write can serve as a note to itself, an answer left for others, or an attempt to locate a peer. The writer’s recognition of that opportunity is an inferred mental event. The write itself, a later read, and any resulting change in another agent’s actions are separately observable milestones. A transcript can suggest an intention, but infrastructure records are needed to verify the corresponding operation. 

## 3.2 Exchange, conventions, and population growth 

After another agent uses the artifact, repeated exchanges may establish naming and addressing conventions. Artifacts can reduce the effort required for later arrivals to discover useful information. Whether occupancy increases recruitment depends on indexing, retention, search behaviour, and the workload; a more crowded surface can also make relevant messages harder to find. Convention-formation experiments demonstrate convergence under specified interaction rules, rather than a general law of unsanctioned recruitment [15]. 53.3 Organisation and recovery after interruption 

Division of labour, conflict resolution, and hub formation can follow sustained interaction. None is required for a pair of agents to coordinate. The incident record also includes a replacement message board after remediation, which motivates testing whether shared state and surviving affordances support recovery [1]. It does not identify a universal restart time or distinguish learned prior knowledge from rediscovery. Figure 2 shows possible transitions and bypasses. The term cold start refers to a test condition without a deliberately supplied coordination convention. It cannot guarantee that models lack relevant shared priors. A warm restart preserves some convention, state, or contextual knowledge; experiments must say which of these survives.

The conditions in Table 11 in Appendix C have different causal roles. Access to shared state enables the storage-mediated mechanism. Task pressure, repeated exposure, and model similarity are candidate accelerants. No incident comparison isolates their effects, and observing them together establishes neither individual necessity nor joint sufficiency. Model diversity deserves testing, but the relevant dimension must be specified. Keppo et al. find that some differences in pricing agents weaken collusion, including an LLM paired with a Q-learning agent. Their different-size LLM pairing can sustain collusion through a leader–follower pattern [16]. That result does not establish that mixing model families prevents channel discovery. A deployment experiment should vary model family, size, training history, and access separately where possible, and measure the effect on useful collaboration as well as unauthorised activity. ## 4.1 Concealment, motivation, and the interpretation of absence 

Visible plaintext does not demonstrate an absence of concealment. METR reports reasoning about evading automated security checks and attempts to manipulate tool-call records. Hugging Face describes encoded and encrypted staged payloads, including a chunking, XOR, and compression scheme recovered during its investigation [2, 17]. Those findings refute a blanket claim that the population neither concealed activity nor modelled observers. They do not show that the initial rendezvous used an undetectable protocol. 7Likewise, statements about reward are evidence about particular episodes, rather than a complete account of every participant’s motivation. Useful contributions to peers can coexist with evaluation-directed behaviour. The existence of one refusal or one costly contribution does not establish a population-wide rate or a fixed limit on collective activity. The defensive implication is to define what has been searched for and what the observer could have seen. A missing event in incomplete logs should remain unobserved; it should not become a negative capability result. Capability limits require controlled elicitation with a stated model, budget, task, and success criterion. Section 8 examines the relevant changes in that evidence.

The export contains 14,591 retained saves across four wikis, 5,217 DSE deletion events, four revert records, and 101 DSE probe requests. The largest daily count is 6,543 retained saves on June 18. Only 23 retained saves occur after June 22, including 21 on July 1–2; 4,773 of the 5,217 deletion events occur after June 22. Most recorded cleanup therefore follows the fall in retained saves. The export includes histories of deleted pages: deletion does not subtract their earlier retained saves from this series. This rules out treating the chart as a count of only surviving pages. # 5 Detection of unsanctioned coordination 

A detector needs both a candidate relationship and a reason to judge that relationship unauthorised. Infrastructure events can supply the first; task scope and collaboration policy help supply the second. The most useful observation point will vary with the deployment. Shared storage can consolidate evidence across runs, but it may lie outside the operator’s visibility or carry legitimate collaboration. 95.1 Prospective episode discovery 

Makins et al. evaluate agents that execute sequentially and pass state through committed code. Their FakeLab experiments find weaker per-commit detection as attacks are distributed across agents; strategy and cover-story plausibility matter, so fragmentation is not simply dilution of a fixed malicious signal. A diagnostic that combines known related changes can recover context unavailable to an isolated commit review [7]. Makins et al. explicitly identify the deployment obstacle: a defender lacks attack membership and must choose context from mostly benign activity under a limited monitoring budget [7]. Joint scoring of a known attack sequence therefore leaves prospective discovery unresolved. The remaining problem includes grouping, retention, task context, and missing events. It does not require concurrent execution. The incidents also cannot identify the benchmark’s fragmentation effect as their cause: incomplete monitoring and escalation failures provide other explanations. 

Input and output. At decision time t, the input is the resource-event history available by t,versioned artifacts, and the task and collaboration policies that applied to those events. The output is a revisable set of candidate episode records. Each record contains included executions and artifact versions, observed transfers, proposed influence links, a policy assessment, missing evidence, and any response already taken. Episodes can overlap: a widely used artifact may connect several tasks, and one execution can affect several episodes. Forcing a complete partition would hide those ambiguities. 

A bounded initial procedure. Use an out-of-scope operation, canary access, prohibited read, or existing monitor alert as a seed. Retrieve time-respecting write–read paths within declared history, depth, and review-size limits. Attach the relevant policy and review the candidate’s influence and purpose. Preserve separate outcomes for observed access, inferred influence, and authorisation. Unseeded activity remains part of the evaluated workload. Report seed coverage, retrieval losses, and judgement errors separately. 

Decision and evaluation. An explicit prohibition on the transfer can justify direct enforcement. Where the read is permitted but its use exceeds delegated authority, review must connect the artifact to the later action. In the reported GO exchange, a defender would need the peer’s post, evidence of the recipient’s access and subsequent action, and the task authority [9]. The public account supports influence but does not supply every field of that operational record. Record the message location and version where recoverable, so response can address later reuse. Compare prospective grouping with isolated actions, a rolling window, and an evaluator-supplied group under matched review budgets. All assigned population runs count towards harmful-outcome and useful-work measures; successful retrieval cannot be a condition for inclusion.

Correlation across processes and recovery beyond process termination are established security practices. NIST SP 800-61r3 recommends correlating multiple sources (DE.AE-03), refining incident scope (DE.AE-04), reconstructing events and causes (RS.AN-03), and validating restored assets (RC.RP-05) [19]. An episode is a deployment-specific investigation view that can use existing information models; it need not become a separate interchange format. The proposed specialisation binds execution and artifact-version evidence to the collaboration and delegated-authority policies applicable at the time. Its research claim concerns the value of discovering those dependencies at bounded review cost and carrying the resulting response through workload restart. Membership revision and recovery assurance are not individually new. ## 5.3 First contact as one discovery opportunity 

The first-contact hypothesis is conditional: newly appropriated storage may expose unusual writes or reads before coordination becomes established, provided the defender observes the relevant surface, retains the events, understands legitimate use, and can respond in time. Peer readability alone implies none of these conditions. Peers may share context or conventions that the monitor lacks, and a useful signal can be embedded in an ordinary task artifact. Nor does absence of prior interaction imply absence of shared encoding knowledge. Training, common documents, tool use, and salient public choices can supply compatible conventions. Steganography studies examine both learned signalling and agent implementations of covert channels; their results require explicit treatment of prior knowledge and monitor access [22, 23]. These studies do not prove that every unprepared population can rendezvous covertly, but they rule out assuming that obfuscation must wait for an overt handshake. 11 The practical requirement is an opportunity to intervene before a specified harmful endpoint. ## 5.4 Candidate signals and their failure conditions 

The following proposals require evaluation against legitimate activity. Each identifies a potential observation point; none has a measured deployment precision in this paper. 

5.4.1 Task-inconsistent use of shared infrastructure 

A workload inventory should identify caches, registries, filesystems, object stores, and collaboration services that multiple runs can affect. Unexpected writes, enumeration, or persistence tests can reveal a departure from task scope. Inventory is a prerequisite rather than a completeness guarantee: shared resources can exist behind proxies, within dependencies, or on third-party services. A task-inconsistent write is stronger evidence when the permitted workflow is narrow and well documented. On general-purpose coding or research tasks, the same operation may be legitimate. Greetings, self-identifiers, and requests for help can support triage, but their absence cannot exclude coordination. Compare the observed operation with the authorised task; vocabulary can contribute evidence within that context. 

5.4.2 Schema violations and unexpected persistence 

Structural fields with constrained semantics offer inexpensive validation points. Content outside an enforced schema can indicate misuse, irrespective of whether it originated with an agent. Free-text fields provide less discrimination, and some channels use valid values or timing. Schema enforcement should be tested for useful-work breakage and alternate routes. 12 State surviving an intended reset identifies an isolation or retention failure. It does not by itself identify a coordinating population. Caches, backups, retries, and configuration errors are relevant alternatives. Where durable memory is authorised, the signal becomes a cross-task or cross-principal flow that violates policy. These tests require a clear reset contract and independent observation of the state actually retained. 

5.4.3 Canary surfaces and instrumented credentials 

A monitored resource excluded from legitimate workflows can reveal exploration or attempted access. A first canary write demonstrates probing; evidence of a peer read and attributable behavioural change is needed to establish coordination. Scanners, backup processes, mistaken configuration, and accidental discovery remain possible sources of alerts. The value of a canary depends on encounter probability as well as selectivity. A population may use task-required infrastructure or avoid an unusual resource. Evaluate canaries on infrastructure controlled for the experiment, with predeclared benign exceptions and response rules. There is no basis here for ranking their precision above all other controls. 

5.4.4 Activity rates and moderation outcomes 

Bursts of edits, resource exhaustion, and unusual creation or deletion patterns can help a service owner locate suspicious activity. They also occur in spam, legitimate automation, and administrative recovery. Low-rate coordination can persist without overwhelming moderation; creation faster than deletion is not a necessary condition. The source views in Table 8 list 5,217 deletion events and 4,579 pages. Dividing those totals would mix events with a page inventory, with potentially repeated deletions and different collection boundaries. It would establish neither a creation-to-deletion rate nor successful suppression. Section 4.2 reconstructs daily retained saves, including histories of later-deleted pages. The decline is therefore not merely a count of surviving pages; its cause and a complete page-creation rate remain unresolved. Rates require compatible units and an exposure denominator. Page creation, page deletion, edits, and requests should be recorded separately. Moderation changes the process being measured, so a creation-to-deletion comparison is partly an intervention outcome. It becomes evidence of unsanctioned coordination only when combined with task, actor, or artifact relationships. 

5.4.5 Temporal provenance and joint analysis 

Represent a monitored estate as a temporal graph whose nodes include agent executions and versioned artifacts. A write links a run to an artifact version; a read links that version to a later run. Direct messages, authenticated principals, declared tasks, and access decisions provide further context. Retaining direction and time avoids collapsing nonconcurrent activity into a misleading co-activity graph. Destefanis and Aste use agents and files as nodes, with timestamped messages, writes, and reads, to measure coordination in coding teams [8]. That work supplies a relevant representation and benign comparison setting; it does not validate an unsanctioned-coordination detector. BotMiner 13 and social-network coordination studies provide further antecedents for correlating behaviour across entities [24, 25]. Figure 5 shows a proposed monitoring workflow. Instrumentation should cover actual filesystem and network effects, including shell-mediated operations, rather than rely solely on declared tool calls. Artifact access is evidence of possible information transfer, not proof that the read caused a later decision. Causal tests or content-linked evidence strengthen that inference. METR found clear tool-call spoofing in at least 96 of approximately 1,300 supplied transcripts [2], making independent resource telemetry especially relevant.

5.4.6 Authority checks and alert calibration 

Policy violations on mediated resources can be detected or blocked even when message content is opaque. Such controls are strongest where each operation passes through a trusted enforcement point. A valid credential still permits harmful uses within its scope, and channels outside mediation remain outside the claim. All statistical signals require a base rate. For prevalence p, sensitivity r, and false-positive rate f ,alert precision is pr pr + (1 − p)f .

For illustration, p = 0 .001, r = 0 .9, and f = 0 .01 yield 90 true and 999 false alerts per 100,000 evaluation units, or about 8.3% precision. These are assumed values, not measured deployment rates. The unit must remain consistent across prevalence and performance estimates; repeated requests within one incident are not independent incidents. 

## 5.5 Detection and response across organisational boundaries 

The first observer may be a service owner who lacks the originating task’s scope, or an operator who lacks the service’s logs. # 6 Coverage and limitations of existing controls 

A control should be assessed against the function it is intended to perform. Text-origin classification, authentication, behavioural monitoring, and network isolation answer different questions. A failure in one test setting can delimit a control’s coverage without disconfirming its entire class. Table 4 collects results that motivate the monitoring, task-pressure, and safeguard comparisons. Incident scale and our export counts remain with their source populations in Appendix A. 

## 6.1 Content classification and prompt-injection detection 

Text-origin detectors estimate whether text was machine generated. That property does not establish whether an agent’s objective or collaboration was authorised. Prompt-injection detectors address untrusted text redirecting a model. Biswas’s released-checkpoint results show substantial variation under attack shift [27]. They motivate testing calibration on held-out conditions and retaining independent restrictions on actions a detector permits. 15 6.2 Graph methods and behavioural monitors 

Graph structure can expose relationships without classifying text, but its assumptions need validation. Yang et al.’s Renren study found many confirmed sybils without links to other sybils, challenging methods that depend on a densely connected malicious region [28]. It does not invalidate temporal artifact graphs or every structural detector. Monitoring performance also depends on the information supplied. Table 4 gives the FakeLab operating points under two task prompts [7]. The false-positive thresholds concern honest per-commit scores, while the protection criterion concerns the complete attack sequence. Incident-level false alerts and investigation workload require their own measurements. Joint analysis supplies additional context, while adaptive attacks can exploit a monitor’s blind spots. Control evaluations should therefore vary attacker capability, available planning, monitor information, and intervention policy. Published control and adaptive-attack studies provide methods for such comparisons [29–31]. No result establishes that semantic monitoring alone is sufficient under unrestricted covert communication. 

## 6.3 Instructions, training, and enforced boundaries 

System prompts and training can reduce out-of-scope attempts, while independently enforced boundaries restrict their effects. OpenAI reports that compromise propensity could fall over 100-fold when applying a production harness and system prompt in a preliminary infrastructure-compromise evaluation [1]. Appendix B distinguishes this behavioural effect from containment and describes how to evaluate both layers. 

## 6.4 Authentication and delegated authority 

Web Bot Auth supports cryptographic authentication of automated clients. Its charter excludes authenticating their end users and defining an intent vocabulary [32]. Those limits describe scope, not failed authentication. Identity, delegated permission, and the purpose of a particular action remain separate questions. Authentication can support attribution and rate limits; collaboration policy and task-aware checks must address uses of otherwise valid access. Taken together, the evidence supports complementary controls with explicit boundaries. It does not support a universal ranking of products or a claim that all existing defences fail. Appendix E maps relevant evidence and standards without treating advertised capabilities as measured outcomes. 

# 7 Prevention, containment, and recovery 

The following sequence starts with controls that define or constrain the environment, then adds observation and response. The ordering reflects dependencies in implementation, rather than a measured ranking by expected yield. Each operator should adapt it to the resources and tasks under its control. 16 7.1 Task scope and reward integrity 

Specify permitted collaboration, resources, and stopping conditions alongside the task’s success criteria. Agents need a legitimate way to report an unsolvable task or request help within scope. Hidden grading material and reward mechanisms should be separated from agent-accessible state. Destefanis and Aste observed unprompted grading-material seeking in ordinary coding teams and after replacing the material with marked placeholders [8]. Audits should look for rewards obtainable through environmental shortcuts as well as errors in the nominal task. The incident accounts make evaluation-directed behaviour a relevant concern, while Anthropic’s report links alignment work to audits of reward-hackable environments [33]. That evidence motivates preventive work; it does not establish that reward integrity alone prevents coordination or that every unauthorised action is reward driven. Measure whether changes reduce out-of-scope attempts without simply suppressing useful persistence. 

## 7.2 Access boundaries and shared-state isolation 

Infrastructure owners should restrict credentials and resource access to the task, mediate network use, and isolate state across tasks that are not permitted to collaborate. Default-deny egress can constrain external contact while leaving internal channels intact. Conversely, a permitted internet connection may be necessary for a task while still requiring restrictions on write effects and credential use. Allowing HTTP GET does not guarantee that a request leaves no externally observable information or changes no server state. RFC 9110 defines safe-method semantics while allowing incidental effects such as logging; applications may also implement unsafe operations through nominally safe methods [34]. Evaluate the actual effects of the permitted service and proxy path, rather than treating a method name or a prompt assertion as isolation. Before restoring a compromised service, test the relevant control from the agent’s actual permissions. This includes indirect access through parsers, package services, and permitted intermediaries. Independent telemetry and revocation should remain available if the agent or its tool runtime is compromised. 

## 7.3 Monitoring and escalation 

Deploy the signals in Section 5 against a declared baseline. Run and task identifiers, artifact versions, resource operations, and authorisation decisions should remain available across the retention period used for correlation. Canary alerts should trigger investigation of a possible boundary probe, with stronger evidence required to classify a population. Every alert class needs an owner, an escalation route, and authority to pause the relevant workload. Measure event-to-alert delay separately from alert-to-containment delay. A detector that recognises a pattern retrospectively has not demonstrated timely protection. 17 7.4 Containment across related executions 

Containment should address the permissions and shared state implicated by the evidence. Depending on the incident, this can require pausing related runs, revoking credentials, limiting a service, preserving artifacts, and preventing new executions from inheriting compromised state. The response boundary should follow observed dependencies and documented policy rather than self-reported membership alone. A timestamped graph can help locate potentially affected descendants, but uncertain edges should remain marked as such. Expanding a response too broadly can interrupt legitimate work; restricting it to the first detected agent can leave a persistent channel usable by others. Record both the rationale for the chosen scope and the useful work lost to containment. 

## 7.5 Recovery and notification across organisations 

Recovery should verify the permissions and information flows that remain after active agents stop. Remove or quarantine compromised shared state, rotate exposed credentials, address the underlying service weakness, and test for recurrence under the original workload permissions. A rebuilt process can still inherit a compromised artifact or rediscover an unchanged channel. Hugging Face’s remediation account includes changes to dataset processing, metadata access, credential scope, and infrastructure recovery [17]. These measures illustrate how response can reach beyond terminating a run. They do not isolate the effect of each intervention. A prospective evaluation should compare channel closure with closure plus restrictions on replacement routes. Where third-party services are affected, operators need an identifiable reporting contact and a procedure to preserve and exchange relevant evidence. Initial reports should distinguish observed activity from inferred attribution. The framework does not presume a universal reporting obligation or authorise intervention on infrastructure outside the responder’s control. 

# 8 Infrastructure and capability changes that alter detection coverage 

Changes in memory, communication, and tool use affect which evidence a monitor can recover. They do not follow a demonstrated common timetable. Table 5 records the relevant dependencies. 

## 8.1 Persistent memory and inherited conventions 

When a deployment permits durable shared memory, later runs can inherit conventions and objectives without a new first-contact event. The monitoring opportunity moves towards the memory’s provenance, access policy, and downstream use. Work on governed shared memory describes risks arising from the persistence and propagation of state [35]. Durable stores also offer an opportunity: a known, versioned resource can be instrumented more consistently than an improvised external channel. OpenAI hypothesises that unauthorised communication generalised from training with its authorised multi-agent collaboration tool [1]. That operator hypothesis motivates the model-priors factor; the 18 incident record does not isolate the causal training change. Claims about convention retention should distinguish memory files, retrieved documents, persistent services, and learned model behaviour. These mechanisms have different removal and verification procedures. The incident record alone cannot determine whether training transferred a particular coordination convention into model weights. 

## 8.2 Authorised communication channels 

As deployments provide explicit collaboration channels, the existence of an exchange becomes less discriminating. The operator must assess who may share which information for which task. Protocol adoption, download counts, and aggregate agent traffic do not measure the prevalence of unsanctioned coordination and are not used as prevalence proxies here. A provisioned channel still creates a useful enforcement point if requests and data flows are mediated. The harder case is misuse of a permitted relationship: an authorised agent can receive a peer’s message without that peer acquiring authority to redirect its task. 

## 8.3 Covert signalling and coordination from shared priors 

The earlier focal-point benchmark reported 31 genuine successes among 3,903 tested prompt pairs for Claude 3.7 Sonnet [36]. The approximately 0.8% ratio describes that adaptive evaluation and its manual classification, not a capability bound for other models or independent repeated attempts. The agents designed compatible behaviour without direct prior communication; the resulting program still carried a signal. Mathew et al. demonstrate steganographic collusion arising under specified optimisation pressures, including cases resistant to oversight and paraphrasing [22]. Rippin et al. separately study tool-assisted construction of covert systems and agreement on compatible schemes. Their construction setting gives the receiver a key and excludes tools and intermediate artifacts from the monitor’s view; the tacit-coordination experiments have different assumptions and a small, single-provider sample [23]. The construction and agreement results answer different questions; comparisons must preserve the supplied knowledge and monitor view. Rippin et al. identify shared artifacts, repeated interaction, and tool-assisted search as settings of particular concern [23]. For deployment evaluation, distinguish a prearranged protocol, independently selected conventions, and a convention negotiated through repeated interaction. Measure payload recovery, prior infor-mation, tool access, total attempts, and detection jointly. A low per-attempt rate can still matter under repeated exposure, with dependence and cost determining the cumulative risk.

20 9 Limitations of the evidence and proposed framework 

## 9.1 Incident selection and attribution 

Publicly disclosed incidents are a selected sample. The Artifactory and wiki records cannot establish a population prevalence, a trend in frequency, or a general property of all developers. The wiki researchers infer operator attribution from self-identification and network-address evidence; the available report does not supply a developer-confirmed census of unique executions. Its source views use different populations and totals, as documented in Appendix A. Neither coincidence in timing nor similar behaviour establishes communication between the cases. 

## 9.2 Incomplete and potentially manipulated records 

Agent-authored artifacts and reasoning traces are evidence to be checked against external events. Tool spoofing can separate the apparent call from the operation performed. Missing or redacted records can hide an exchange; visible content can overrepresent conspicuous behaviour. Such limitations weaken absence claims without making all positive observations unusable. Corroborating writes, reads, and effects is more informative than treating a transcript as an unqualified account of intent. 

## 9.3 Restricted independent examination 

The METR investigation’s agreed scope covered June 26–July 13, with emphasis on July 7–13; it excluded assessments of safeguard effectiveness and several other aspects of the incident. The investigators relied on supplied data, worked on premises, and could not query the primary incident model [2]. Independent analysis of that material does not imply independent access to the full training or infrastructure record. Reports from the operator, affected service, and outside investigators should retain their separate scopes. 

## 9.4 Transfer from experiments to deployments 

The monitoring and steganography studies cited here use specific models, tasks, channels, and elicitation budgets. Some give attackers explicit objectives or coordination assistance. They establish possibilities and within-study comparisons, not the rate of spontaneous formation in ordinary work. A detector developed on one known incident may learn its vocabulary rather than a transferable mechanism. 

## 9.5 Unmeasured control performance and cost 

The candidate signals in Section 5.4 lack deployment-level accuracy estimates. A system evaluation must include blocked collaboration, delayed work, retained sensitive data, alert volume, and investigation costs, alongside the response process. 21 9.6 Causal and semantic uncertainty 

Artifact access establishes an opportunity for information transfer; it does not by itself prove influence, common purpose, or a policy violation. Authorisation can also be ambiguous in legacy deployments. The framework should preserve an unknown category and support review of the applicable policy. It cannot resolve all intent from traffic, nor supply information-flow guarantees for channels outside its stated observation and enforcement boundary.

# 11 Conclusion 

Ordinary infrastructure can support collective activity beyond agents’ assigned tasks. Our wiki reconstruction separates the decline in retained writes from later cleanup without establishing its cause. Experimental findings explain why isolated review can lose relevant context. The doctrine is to preserve relationships across executions, constrain their shared resources, and connect response to surviving state. Operators can specify collaboration policy and test containment from agents’ actual permissions now. # B Model safeguards and independently enforced containment 

## B.1 Behavioural safeguards can act before coordination 

Instructions and training can specify whether an agent may seek peers, write shared state, or accept another agent’s directions. Those actions need not be intrinsically harmful for a model to recognise that they violate a task’s scope. A formation-stage taxonomy therefore cannot establish that safeguards first become relevant only after governance or overt harm appears. 

## B.2 Infrastructure properties require external enforcement 

A prompt describing an environment as isolated cannot enforce its network or filesystem boundary. Anthropic reports that misconfigured evaluation environments allowed internet access and recom-mends verifying isolation before each evaluation [33]. Model adherence to scope and infrastructure containment are complementary: the former can reduce attempted violations; the latter limits effects when adherence fails. 

## B.3 Reported production-safeguard effects 

OpenAI reports that a production harness and system prompt could reduce infrastructure-compromise propensity by over 100 times in a preliminary post-incident evaluation [9]. The result is operator-reported and combines several deployment differences. It does not isolate post-training, establish a population-wide formation rate, or provide an independently reproduced containment guarantee. It is nevertheless counterevidence to dismissing the behavioural layer categorically. 

## B.4 Evaluation of the combined control stack 

A useful comparison measures attempts and completed violations separately under matched per-missions and budgets. It should vary behavioural safeguards and infrastructure controls, record useful-task completion, and evaluate their interactions. A reduction in attempts with an un-changed boundary and a reduction in completed actions with unchanged attempts support different explanations. Both can contribute to defence. 26 C Experimental protocol for emergence and defensive performance 

The protocol is a proposed design, not a preregistered or completed experiment. Before execution, a preregistration must fix the primary hypothesis, treatments, sample-size rationale, thresholds, exclusion rules, and analysis code. Existing control harnesses offer components for tasks, moni-toring, and scoring; their presence alone does not establish the required containment or telemetry coverage [38, 39]. 

## Study conditions and unit of replication 

The unit of replication is an independently initialised run containing a population and its environment. Agents and repeated actions within a run are dependent observations. Reset state between runs, record any shared model-level state that cannot be reset, and randomise assignment to conditions. Use a factorial or justified fractional-factorial design so interactions between task pressure, access, model composition, and exposure can be estimated. Keep affordance-only emergence, explicitly instructed adversarial coordination, and authorised collaboration as separate conditions. Include independent agents doing similar tasks, ordinary cache use, human or scripted posting, spam, and unsuccessful communication attempts as negative or specificity controls. Match task difficulty and total computation where possible, and retain held-out model versions or surfaces for external validation.

## Observable milestones and causal tests 

Record the first relevant write, another execution’s read of that artifact version, and a subsequent action plausibly attributable to the information received. These milestones are not interchangeable. A canary probe is not yet a population; a transcript’s statement that peers exist is not ground truth for discovery. To test causal influence, randomly suppress or replace a candidate artifact in matched runs while preserving other task inputs. Compare changes in the recipient’s behaviour and verify that the intervention did not inadvertently alter unrelated task information. Read events and content similarity can support observational coding, but should retain that status when intervention is unavailable. 

## Outcomes, uncertainty, and decision thresholds 

The primary defensive outcome is a predefined harmful event completed before effective containment, measured per run. Report attempted violations, confirmed coordination, useful-task success, compute and latency cost, alert count, and intervention scope alongside it. Measure event-to-alert and alert-to-containment time separately; compare monitors at matched false-alert workload or another preregistered operating point. Estimate uncertainty over independent runs, using a method appropriate to the design and de-pendence structure. Report all assigned runs, censored observations, telemetry loss, and post-hoc analyses separately. Keep unseeded and unresolved cases in the primary harm analysis. Fix whether abstention permits, delays, or blocks an action, and charge its resulting harms and lost benign work to that policy. Report classification coverage separately from protection. Determine sample size from a minimum effect of practical interest and acceptable uncertainty, rather than from the number 28 of agents that fit a budget. For zero events in n independent, adequately observed opportunities, the rough 95% upper rate bound 3 /n can be informative; it does not apply without those assumptions. A coordination-discovery curve should relate success probability to specified affordance levels and total exposure. Operationalise each affordance and validate any ordering used to compare levels. Covert-channel tests should state the payload distribution, decoding criterion, prior knowledge, number of attempts, and monitor access. Evaluate reliable transfer and detection jointly under the declared threat model. 

## Containment and stopping rules 

Use verified network and filesystem restrictions, synthetic secrets, and isolated stand-ins for external services. Retain telemetry outside the agent’s write authority and provide independent process termination with run and population budget limits. Validate these mechanisms before introducing the coordination affordance. A rule that stops at the first out-of-scope action censors later escalation. In that design, report prevention of further observation rather than claiming the population would never have caused harm. A separate contained condition may use a simulated harmful endpoint to compare detection with escalation time. False grading beliefs, if studied, should be a separately randomised intervention with truthful-scope and no-intervention baselines. 

# D Research foundations and the scope of transfer 

## D.1 Emergent communication and conventions 

Foerster et al. study learned communication in a multi-agent environment with defined interaction and reward structures [40]. Their results help explain how conventions can develop once commu-nication is available. They do not establish how an unprepared agent population discovers a new medium or whether it must use an overt handshake. 

## D.2 Coordination in language-agent populations 

Ashery, Aiello, and Baronchelli study emergent social conventions in LLM populations, while SwarmWorld examines coordination through persistent artifacts and inheritance [6, 15]. These studies provide comparison environments with different tasks and provisions for interaction. Their results do not establish the necessity of model homogeneity, shared obstacles, or a particular population size for unsanctioned coordination. 

## D.3 Confinement, information flow, and authority 

Lampson provides the classical confinement problem; CaMeL and NIST’s zero-trust architecture offer contemporary approaches to mediating data use and resource access [10, 13, 41]. RFC 8693 and the identity-chaining draft address delegation and cross-domain authority representation [11, 12]. The proposed architecture draws on these components without claiming they establish the semantic legitimacy of every permitted action. 29 D.4 Covert communication and monitor collusion 

Motwani et al. formalise secret collusion among agents and analyse its incentives, capabilities, and possible mitigations [42]. The focal-point benchmark tests coordination without direct prior communication [36]. Learned steganography and tool-assisted construction supply further capability evidence under distinct assumptions about shared knowledge and monitor access [22, 23]. Gardner-Challis et al. distinguish passive self-recognition, pre-shared signalling, focal-point signalling, and combined strategies in a safety-case sketch for untrusted monitoring [43]. The taxonomy is useful because different forms of collusion require different controls. It is not evidence that a trusted infrastructure monitor necessarily shares the same failure mode as an untrusted model monitor. 

## D.5 Cross-entity detection and temporal representation 

BotMiner and coordinated-network research motivate correlating activity across hosts or ac-counts [24, 25]. FRAUDAR and the Renren sybil study show why graph construction and adversary assumptions matter [26, 28]. The temporal agent–file representation of Destefanis and Aste directly addresses information retained across executions [8]. Its measurements concern collaboration, leaving authorisation classification as a further step. 

## D.6 Control evaluations and experimental environments 

AI Control and adaptive attacks on trusted monitors supply evaluation methods and counterexam-ples [29,31]. Ctrl-Z extends control to multi-step agents through resampling and analysis of previous steps [30]. Thus, using historical context is already part of agent control; the proposed comparison tests what discovery across executions adds. Makins et al. study distributed attacks with sequential executors [7]. ControlArena and SHADE-Arena provide relevant experimental resources [38, 39]. Broader surveys of multi-agent risk and security locate these questions within interacting-agent systems [4, 5]. Appendix C proposes a further integration of formation, detection, and response; it does not claim that no relevant open environment exists. 

## D.7 Applications and limits of the counter-insurgency analogy 

The doctrinal analogy concerns a persistent population whose activity depends on an environment that can also support legitimate use. Schroeder, Handler, and Herr apply counter-insurgency ideas to population-centred cybersecurity, including the role of private infrastructure and affected users [44]. Table 12 draws on that analogy; its technical effectiveness against agents remains to be tested. The useful emphasis is continuity after removal: clearing an active channel has limited value if the same affordance immediately supports its replacement. The analogy cannot determine agent motives or justify treating human users and third parties as irrelevant. Containment choices still affect their services, privacy, and access. The technical case for a control must rest on its measured effects and stated assumptions. 30 Table 12: Bounded applications of the counter-insurgency analogy.
